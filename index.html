Toronto urban wood waste: mulch vs biochar

Spatial life-cycle and techno-economic model by Yiduo Zhou. Python, SQL (DuckDB), GeoPandas, NetworkX, SciPy, SALib. Aggregated, ward-level results only.

Economics here are illustrative: capex, opex and biochar price are literature-style ranges, not quotes. Supply is a modelled index validated against 311 requests (leave-one-out R² 0.65), not measured tonnage.
Ward colour = share of citywide street-tree basal area (wood-waste supply proxy). Circles = transfer stations (candidate biochar sites); click one. Station colour = mean haul km to that site.

Limits, stated plainly

The nearest-station assumption failed validation against 2023 weigh-scale data (Spearman −0.43, n = 6), so this tool does not claim savings from re-routing existing flows; it ranks siting options only. Speeds are assumed by road class (no city-wide traffic data). Siting barely changes the break-even price (about $58–66 per tCO₂e across stations); capex, opex and biochar price matter far more. Mulch decomposition emissions are set to zero. Moisture basis for the sequestration credit is a key v1 correction (credit applied per dry tonne). Data: City of Toronto Open Data (wards, street trees, 311, Centreline, transfer stations, green spaces) and City staff weigh-scale/haul records, aggregated with permission.
